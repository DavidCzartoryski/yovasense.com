Nothing crashes. Every step just gets slower.

Distributed training moves in lockstep: a step finishes when the slowest rank arrives. One degraded GPU, link, or host sets the pace for the entire job — while utilization still looks busy and nothing raises an error.

So teams find out late, or not at all. The run finishes hours behind, and the investigation starts from logs, across the infrastructure, network, storage, and hardware teams.

  • Throughput drops a few percent mid-run — and stays down
  • GPU utilization looks fine; the job is slow anyway
  • No error names the GPU, link, or host that changed

From slowdown to verified recovery, in one loop.

  1. 1

    Detect

    YovaSense times every rank at the collectives it already joins, and compares each rank against its peers and its own history. A persistent slowdown is flagged while the job is still running — not in a postmortem.

  2. 2

    Confirm

    Before anyone acts, YovaSense re-tests under matched conditions: same workload, hardware, and topology. A one-off fluctuation doesn't become an incident, and an unconfirmed regression never becomes the new baseline. The same matched testing runs after driver, library, or configuration changes.

  3. 3

    Localize

    Evidence across the host and input pipeline, the GPU, the communication runtime, and the interconnect narrows the problem to the rank, node, link, or software change responsible — with the measurements behind the conclusion, including when the evidence isn't enough to call it.

  4. 4

    Act

    Within the policies you set: rebalance work away from the slow rank, recommend a targeted fix, or apply a supported correction. Anything that needs a restart, a repair, or your approval is escalated — never hidden.

  5. 5

    Verify

    YovaSense re-measures useful training throughput after the action. If it didn't recover, you see that too — and reversible actions roll back.

Inside YovaSense: the architecture and research

The slowdowns that don't crash anything.

GPU degradation

Thermal throttling, power caps, clocks that stop boosting.

Communication

A degraded port, link, or switch path; one island's collectives slowing down.

Host and input

CPU contention, or a data-loader or storage stall holding one rank back off the GPU.

Change regressions

Slowdowns that arrive with a driver, communication-library, or configuration change.

Not another dashboard to watch.

Monitoring tells you something is slow. YovaSense closes the loop: it confirms the slowdown is real, localizes it, acts within your policies, and proves the recovery. The output isn't a chart — it's an incident with a cause, the evidence for it, the action taken, and the measured result.
