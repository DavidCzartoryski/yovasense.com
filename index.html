Beyond monitoring

Monitoring raises the alert. YovaSense drives it to resolution.

Monitoring tells your team that a GPU disappeared or a job failed. Finding out why still takes an engineer, pulling evidence by hand from the scheduler, the servers, the GPUs, the network and the job.

What monitoring tells you

  • A node is out of service.
  • A GPU stopped reporting.
  • A network link keeps dropping.
  • A job failed.

What YovaSense adds

  • One incident instead of four alerts.
  • A named GPU and its likely cause.
  • Capacity back in service, verified.
  • A technician ticket only when needed.

How it works

From the first alert to verified recovery.

Every incident runs the same three steps, day or night.

  1. 01

    Find the root cause

    YovaSense reads your GPUs, servers, network, scheduler and workloads together. A burst of alerts becomes one incident with a named cause.

    GPUs · servers · network · scheduler · workloads

  2. 02

    Automate the fix

    It runs the recovery your policy allows and asks a person before anything beyond it. Nothing closes until YovaSense has checked that the capacity is healthy again.

    reset · restart · reschedule · verify

  3. 03 · to a person

    Escalate what remains

    Anything that needs hands on hardware becomes a prioritized ticket, with the evidence and the repair steps attached.

    → An engineer in the loop. Not an engineer on every alert.

What it handles

Six families of infrastructure incidents.

The same three steps run for every one, across training and inference.

  • GPU hardware failures

    Faulty GPUs, memory errors and GPUs that drop off the node.

  • Network and interconnect

    Slow or failing links between GPUs, servers and racks.

  • Performance degradation

    Jobs that slow down without failing: stragglers, throttling, regressions.

  • Drivers and services

    The drivers, firmware and services every GPU depends on.

  • Unavailable GPU nodes

    Nodes pulled out of service, and the capacity stranded with them.

  • Power and cooling

    Thermal and power faults, from one GPU to a whole rack.

FAQ

Common questions.

What does YovaSense take off my team's plate?

The investigation and the routine recovery. YovaSense works out why GPU capacity went down, runs the fixes your policy allows and checks that the capacity is healthy again, so your engineers only see the incidents that need them, with the evidence already gathered.

Does it replace our monitoring?

No. YovaSense works on top of the monitoring, schedulers and logs you already run. Monitoring raises the alert, and YovaSense carries it through to a verified recovery.

How much does it do on its own?

As much as you allow. Your policy sets which recovery actions run automatically, anything beyond it waits for a person to approve, and a limit caps how much capacity YovaSense can take out of service by itself.

How does it work with Slack?

YovaSense posts each incident to the channel you choose, with the root cause and the proposed fix. Your team approves or escalates from the message, and the thread keeps a record of every step through to recovery.

What infrastructure does it support?

NVIDIA and AMD GPUs, Slurm and Kubernetes, training and inference, on clusters you own or rent. One deployment covers several clusters, and each keeps its own policy.

Where does our data go?

YovaSense runs in your environment and reads operational telemetry, not your models or datasets. Incident updates go only to the Slack channels and ticketing tools you connect, and it has only the access you grant it.

Couldn't a general AI assistant do this?

Because diagnosing a GPU fleet takes context a chat agent doesn't have: how every job maps to its GPUs, servers, network links and racks, and what each kind of failure looks like across them. YovaSense pairs known failure signatures with AI reasoning, and verifies every fix before it closes an incident.

How do we evaluate it?

Write to us and we'll start with your fleet and the incidents that take the most of your team's time. YovaSense connects to the telemetry you already collect, and you decide what it may do on its own before it does anything.
